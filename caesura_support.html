Frequently Asked Questions

How do eye breaks work?

By default, every 20 minutes a calm full-screen overlay asks you to look at something far away for 20 seconds, the 20-20-20 rule. You can change the interval and the length in Settings.

What is a rest break?

Caesura counts the time you actually spend working. After a long stretch (two hours by default) it suggests a longer rest, 20 minutes by default. Idle time and a locked screen do not count as work.

Can I see a break coming?

Yes. A small banner counts down before each break, so nothing catches you mid-sentence. You can snooze, pause or skip a break from the menu bar.

Why did my break not start during a call?

While a microphone, camera or fullscreen video is in use, Caesura holds the countdowns and the menu says why, for example which app is using the microphone. When the call ends, the countdowns continue and a break that was due starts then. Caesura only checks whether these devices are busy; it never listens or records. You can turn this off in the menu bar.

What does eye tracking do?

Eye tracking is optional and off by default. When you turn it on, the break countdown pauses while you are still looking at the screen and continues once you look away. Camera frames are processed on your Mac and are never stored or sent anywhere.

Eye tracking does not work. What should I check?

Make sure Caesura has camera access in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera, and that your face is visible and reasonably lit.

What is strict mode?

With strict mode on, an eye break cannot be skipped until its countdown has finished. Turn it on or off in Settings.

Where are my statistics?

Open the menu bar menu to see today's breaks, and open the statistics window from there for completed and skipped breaks per day and over the last weeks. Statistics are stored only on your Mac.

Which macOS version do I need?

macOS 15.5 or later.
